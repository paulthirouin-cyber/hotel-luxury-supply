# Custom Leather Goods for Hotels & Suites: The Complete Buyer's Guide

Leather goods are the details a director of rooms notices first and a guest notices last, which is exactly the point. A valet tray on the nightstand, a tissue box cover that matches the headboard, a cable snap on the desk: individually quiet, together they make a suite feel finished rather than furnished. This guide covers the six pieces in the HL Supply collection, the deboss finish, color and material choices, and the practical numbers behind an in-room leather program.

## Where leather goods work on a property

- **Suites and guestrooms.** Valet trays, tissue box covers, and cable snaps as permanent room fixtures in the property's palette.
- **Bathrooms and spa suites.** Moisture-resistant boxes for amenities; structured trays for towels and bottles.
- **In-room dining and butler service.** Structured serving trays for amenities, tea service, and fine-dining accessories.
- **Arrival and VIP gifts.** Luggage tags and cable snaps as take-home pieces that travel with the guest.
- **Boutique retail.** Tags and trays sold as a coordinated home collection.

## Debossing: the finish and why it suits a suite

A debossed logo is pressed down into the leather, leaving a recessed impression with no ink. In a room full of surfaces that compete for attention, a deboss reads as texture rather than branding: visible when the light rakes across it, invisible from across the room. That restraint is why it is the standard finish across the collection. Three practical notes:

1. **Vector artwork.** The deboss die is cut from the logo paths, so supply AI, EPS, SVG, or vector PDF files. Very fine lines fill in at small scale; the mockup shows how the crest will read at tray or tag size.
2. **Blind deboss is standard.** No foil, no color. For a bespoke piece, a tonal or foil finish can be explored, and embossing (a raised logo) is possible where the design suits it.
3. **Placement is confirmed on the proof.** Centered on a tray base, on the tag face, on the box lid, or offset to a corner for a quieter effect.

## Color: match the room, not the logo

The strongest leather programs choose a color from the interior scheme, a warm sand, a deep green, a navy, rather than the brightest brand color. The deboss carries the identity; the color carries the room. Every piece is produced in any custom color, so share Pantone references or an interior finish sample with the brief. The proof confirms the shade on the actual material, which matters because leather finishes shift between matte and sheen.

## Writing the brief

A leather brief starts with the room, not the product: which pieces, in which rooms, in what color, matched to which interior finish. List the suite types and the room count per type so quantities fall out naturally, then add spares for housekeeping replacement. Send the logo in vector format and state whether the deboss should be centered or offset, and whether the same crest is used across every piece or a simpler mark is preferred on the smaller ones (the cable snap and the luggage tag have less surface than a tray).

Color is the decision most worth taking slowly. Share a Pantone reference or, better, a physical finish sample from the interior scheme: a headboard fabric, a drapery, a wood stain. The proof confirms the match on the actual vegan leather, where matte and sheen can shift a shade. If the program accompanies a renovation, brief it alongside the interiors so the leather color is chosen from the same palette rather than approximated afterwards.

## Common mistakes, and how to avoid them

- **Matching the logo color instead of the room.** A bright brand color on a nightstand fights the interior. Choose a tone from the room and let the deboss carry the identity.
- **Mixing projects.** Trays ordered one year and tissue covers the next in a slightly different shade read as mismatched. Specify the full set in one quote, even if deliveries are staged.
- **Over-detailed debossing.** Fine lines fill in when pressed into leather at tag scale. Approve the simplified mark shown on the mockup.
- **No spares.** Housekeeping cycles are hard on small items. Order replacement stock at the first tier rather than reordering a handful later.
- **Forgetting the take-home piece.** The luggage tag is the one item that travels. Placing it in the valet tray at arrival turns a fixture into a gift.
