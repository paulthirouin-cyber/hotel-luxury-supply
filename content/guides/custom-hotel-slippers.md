# Custom Hotel Slippers: The Complete Buyer's Guide

Slippers are the amenity guests remember after checkout. They are worn at the most private moments of a stay, in the spa, after a bath, at turndown, and they are the one item almost every guest takes home. That makes them the most repeated expression of a property's identity, and the one where a cheap pair does the most damage. This guide covers the four textures in the HL Supply collection, the embroidery decisions, the quantities a property-wide program needs, and how to schedule it.

## Where slippers work on a property

- **Turndown and suite amenity.** A pair placed at turndown or in the wardrobe at arrival, replaced for each stay.
- **Spa and wellness.** Issued with the robe at the spa desk; often a lighter, more breathable style than the suite pair.
- **Pool and beach club.** For properties where guests walk between the room and the deck, paired with flip-flops from the [flip-flops collection](category:flip-flops).
- **VIP and wellness retreats.** A premium pair in a presentation pouch as part of a coordinated suite set.

## Choosing the texture

**Terry fleece (Cloud)** is the classic. Plush, absorbent, and in the same family as the property's towels, it signals spa in a single glance. The Cloud adds an anti-slip sole and is machine washable, which matters for properties that launder and reissue rather than treat slippers as single-use.

**Waffle weave (Lattice)** is lighter and more breathable, with a raised grid texture that looks refined in a warm-climate suite and dries quickly after a poolside walk. Reinforced stitching keeps the lightweight build durable.

**Ribbed knit (Linear)** is the contemporary option. The linear texture reads as fashion rather than bathrobe, the cushioned footbed is comfortable on stone floors, and it suits a design-led property whose interiors are architectural rather than plush.

**Biodegradable fibers (Verdant)** are for properties with a sustainability standard to meet. The Verdant is crafted from sustainable materials, is biodegradable, and still carries an embroidered logo and a custom color, so the eco choice does not look like a compromise.

Many properties run two textures: a Cloud or Linear in the suite and a Lattice in the spa.

## Embroidery on slippers

Logos are embroidered on the vamp, the upper that faces the guest when the slipper is on the floor. Because the fabric is textured, embroidery decisions matter more than on a cap:

1. **Tone-on-tone is the luxury default.** Thread one shade off the slipper color reads as a woven detail rather than a label. Properties that want the logo to be seen in photographs choose a contrast thread instead.
2. **Simplify the mark.** Terry and waffle textures soften fine detail; a wordmark or a simple crest embroiders more cleanly than a detailed emblem. The mockup shows the result on the actual texture.
3. **Supply vector artwork** for clean digitizing, and share Pantone references for both the slipper color and the thread.

## Minimums, tiers, and sizing a program

Slipper styles start at **1,000 pairs**, with quantity pricing tiers at 1,000, 2,500, 5,000, and 10,000 pairs. The minimum reflects how slippers are used: as a property-wide amenity replaced each stay. A simple way to size the first order is keys multiplied by expected stays over the restocking period, plus spa issuance. A 150-key resort at 70% occupancy with three-night average stays replaces roughly 1,000 pairs a month in suites alone, which is why most programs order at the 2,500 or 5,000 tier and schedule staged deliveries. ## Writing the brief

A slipper brief covers the texture (or textures) per program, the slipper color and thread color with Pantone references, the logo in vector format with a preferred size on the vamp, the size run and expected mix, the quantity per program with a restocking rhythm, and the date the first delivery is needed. State whether pairs are single-use or laundered and reissued, because that decides the texture (the machine-washable Cloud suits reissue) and the packaging (a simple wrap for turndown, a pouch for VIP suites).

If the program is property-wide, give the concierge the key count, occupancy, and average length of stay. Those three numbers size the first order and the staged deliveries far more reliably than a guess, and they determine which quantity tier the program should be quoted at. A spa issuance estimate (treatments per day, pairs per treatment) is added on top.

## Common mistakes, and how to avoid them

- **Choosing by catalog photo rather than climate.** Terry in a tropical suite is warm; waffle weave in a mountain spa is thin. Match texture to the setting and run two textures if the property has both.
- **A detailed crest on a textured vamp.** Terry and waffle soften fine lines. A wordmark or a simplified crest embroiders cleanly; the mockup shows the difference.
- **Ordering a season at once with nowhere to store it.** Staged deliveries exist for this. Order at the right tier and schedule shipments to the restocking rhythm.
- **Ignoring sizes.** A single mid size leaves a share of guests without a fitting pair. Share the expected mix; amenity programs usually need fewer sizes than retail, but more than one.
- **Forgetting the sole.** Stone and tile floors need an anti-slip sole; it is a safety specification, not a comfort one. Confirm it on the proof.

## Pairing slippers with the rest of the suite

Slippers rarely stand alone. The strongest turndown presentations pair them with a sleep mask from the accessories collection in the same color and thread, placed together on the bed or in a valet tray from the leather goods collection. Spa programs pair the Lattice with a robe and a bottle at the treatment lounge; beach properties pair a suite slipper with a flip-flop in the same shade so the guest walks from the room to the deck in one identity. Quoting the set together keeps colors, embroidery, and delivery dates aligned, and it usually moves at least one piece into a better tier. The concierge can build the set in a single project from the quote cart.
