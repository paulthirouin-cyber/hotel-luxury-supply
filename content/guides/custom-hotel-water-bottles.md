# Custom Hotel Water Bottles: The Complete Buyer's Guide

Properties are replacing single-use plastic in rooms, spas, and fitness spaces, and the reusable bottle has become part of the guest experience rather than a giveaway. Done well, it sits on a nightstand like an object that belongs there, goes to the gym and the pool, and leaves in a suitcase with the property's name etched into it. This guide covers the sizes, construction, logo method, and program decisions behind a custom bottle for a hotel, resort, or spa.

## Where bottles live on a property

- **Suites and guestrooms.** A bottle at turndown or beside a filtered-water station replaces plastic and reads as a considered amenity.
- **Spa and wellness.** Bottles for treatment lounges, hydrotherapy circuits, and yoga or fitness classes.
- **Fitness and outdoor programs.** Guided hikes, cycling, tennis, and the gym floor.
- **Retail.** The boutique bottle in the property's signature color is a reliable seller alongside caps and totes.
- **Events and conferences.** A bottle in the event colorway for a retreat or an owners' meeting, often paired with a tote.

## 350 ml or 500 ml?

Both sizes are available on every style, and most programs choose by setting rather than preference.

- **350 ml** suits the suite and the spa: it fits a nightstand and a treatment-room shelf, weighs less when full, and matches the quantity a guest drinks in one sitting. It is also the better retail size for a slim shelf presentation.
- **500 ml** suits fitness, outdoor, and all-day use: the gym floor, a guided hike, a day at the beach club, a conference bag.

Properties running both a suite amenity and a fitness program often specify the same style in both sizes so the identity stays consistent.

## Insulation and materials

All four bottles use vacuum insulation: a vacuum between inner and outer walls that slows heat transfer so water stays cold and coffee stays hot for hours. The Marquis and the Aura specify triple-wall construction; the Aura is built from 304 stainless steel, the food-grade alloy that resists corrosion and does not hold flavors. The Vanguard is built for knocks with a rugged body and a stainless swing handle; the Lumière trades bulk for a slim profile with an accent wrist strap.

Finish is part of the material story. The Marquis's soft-touch finish is matte and warm in the hand; the Aura's seamless lines and flush-mount lid give it the architectural look of a carafe. Custom colors apply to the body coating, and the proof confirms the shade on the actual finish.

## Laser etching: why it is the right logo method

Logos on HL Supply bottles are laser-etched rather than printed. The laser removes or marks the top coating, revealing the steel beneath, so the mark is permanent: it does not chip, scratch off, or fade in a dishwasher, and it needs no ink that could wear away on a bottle used daily. Etching also suits the quiet aesthetic of a luxury property; the logo reads as tone-on-tone metal rather than a printed label. Supply vector artwork; the etch reproduces fine line work well, and the mockup shows scale and placement before the proof locks it.

Placement options include the front of the body at label height, a lower offset for a small mark, or the lid where the design allows. A secondary line (the property name, a wellness program, an event date) can sit beneath the crest.

## Color

Every style is produced in any custom color: a matte neutral that recedes into a suite, a saturated accent that anchors a retail shelf, or the property's signature shade across all programs. Share Pantone references with the brief. Because the etch reveals the steel, the body color also determines how much contrast the logo has, and the proof is the place to judge it.

## Writing the brief

A bottle brief covers five things: the style (or styles), the size in 350 ml or 500 ml, the body color with a Pantone reference, the logo in vector format with a preferred placement, and the quantity per program with a delivery date. If the bottles serve more than one program, say so; a suite amenity, a spa issue, and a boutique run are often best quoted together so tiers are reached sooner and colors stay consistent across the property. If the program replaces single-use plastic, note the room count and expected replacement rhythm so the first order and any restock can be sized sensibly.

The mockup stage is where a bottle program either looks like an object or like a giveaway. Ask to see the etch at true scale on the chosen body color: a crest that is confident on a white mockup can disappear on a dark matte body, and a wordmark that fits a 500 ml bottle may crowd a 350 ml one. Approve placement and scale on the proof for each size separately.

## Common mistakes, and how to avoid them

- **Printing instead of etching.** A printed logo on a bottle that lives in a dishwasher or a gym bag wears within a season. Laser etching is permanent, which is why it is the catalog standard.
- **One size for every program.** The suite wants 350 ml; the gym wants 500 ml. Specifying both sizes of one style keeps the identity consistent without compromising either use.
- **Choosing the logo color before the body color.** The etch reveals the steel beneath the coating, so contrast depends on the body color. Decide the body first, then judge the mark on the proof.
- **Forgetting the lid.** The Aura's flush-mount lid and the Marquis's carry loop are part of the silhouette; make sure the mockup shows the bottle closed, as it will sit on the nightstand.
- **Under-ordering for suites.** A property-wide amenity needs spares for loss and replacement. Plan for them in the first run rather than paying a lower tier twice.
