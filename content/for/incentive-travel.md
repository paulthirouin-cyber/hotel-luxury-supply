# Custom Room Drop Gifts and Welcome Kits for Incentive Travel Programs

An incentive trip is a reward, and the gifts are the part of the reward the qualifier takes home. The planners who run these programs, at incentive houses, destination management companies, and corporate event agencies, know that a bag of branded leftovers reads as cheap and a well-made object with a quiet mark reads as earned. This page covers what incentive programs commission from HL Supply when they land at a luxury property: the welcome kit, the nightly room drop, the co-branding hierarchy, and the quantities and timelines that fit a program calendar. HL Supply works with these programs as a welcome inbound partner; the property's collection is the starting point and the program's mark is applied with restraint.

## The room drop is the format

Incentive programs gift differently from conferences. Instead of one registration bag, the group receives a welcome kit on arrival and then a small gift in the room each night: the room drop. That format favors a specific set of pieces: small, finishable in the property's colors, light enough to pack, and good enough to keep. It also favors one vehicle. [The Petite Mini Tote](product:petite-mini-tote) is the room-drop carrier in the catalog: it holds a night's gift, stands on a bed or a desk, and is used afterwards for the beach, the pool, and the flight home. Brief one Petite per qualifier per drop, or one per stay with a new gift inside each night.

## The welcome kit

Arrival night sets the tone. A kit that works usually holds four things: the carrier, something to wear, something for the room, and something for the flight.

- **The carrier:** [The Signature Solid Tote](product:signature-tote) in the property's colors for a beach or resort program, or [The Petite Mini Tote](product:petite-mini-tote) where the group travels light.
- **To wear:** [The Regent Classic Cap](product:regent-classic-cap) for a polished group, [The Heritage Dad Hat](product:heritage-dad-hat) for a relaxed one.
- **For the room:** [The Concierge Valet Tray](product:concierge-valet-tray) debossed with the property crest, the piece that stays on the nightstand all week and goes home in the suitcase.
- **For the flight:** [The Solace Sleeping Mask](product:solace-sleeping-mask) and [The Clarity Lens Cloth](product:clarity-lens-cloth), the two pieces that are used before the group has left the airport.

## Nightly room drops

A four-night program typically runs three drops after the welcome kit, each in a Petite:

1. **Night two:** [The Wayfarer Luggage Tag](product:wayfarer-luggage-tag), debossed with the property crest, with a card naming the next day's excursion.
2. **Night three:** [The Lumière Bottle](product:lumiere-bottle), slim with an accent wrist strap, for the day on the water or the course.
3. **Night four:** [The Link Leather Cable Snap](product:link-cable-snap) for the desk after the trip, the piece that outlasts the program.

For a wellness-led program, [The Zenith Yoga Mat](product:zenith-yoga-mat) at a 100-unit minimum is a realistic group gift for a morning session on the terrace, and [The Canopy Premium Umbrella](product:canopy-premium-umbrella) is the drop that gets a laugh and gets used at a coastal property. The pieces that start at 100 units, hats, leather goods, and the yoga mat, are the ones that fit most incentive group sizes without surplus.

## Co-branding: the property first, the program second

Two marks on one object need a hierarchy, and incentive programs usually have three parties who want to be seen: the host property, the sponsoring company, and sometimes the agency. The pattern that works: the property's crest in its standard position (front of the cap, front of the tote, debossed on the leather, etched on the bottle) and the program's mark small and secondary, on the side of the cap, inside the tote, or as a second line beneath the etch. The agency and the trip name belong on the printed card inside the Petite, not on the goods. Every piece is produced in any custom color, so the program's color can be carried by the kit while the logos stay restrained; the [logo methods comparison](guide:embroidery-vs-deboss-vs-laser-etching) explains how embroidery, deboss, and etching each handle a second mark.

## Quantities and tiers

Incentive groups typically run 50 to 250 qualifiers plus partners and staff, which sits at or near the catalog's lowest minimums: hats and leather goods start at 100 units with tiers at 100, 250, 500, and 1,000; bags and bottles at 200 with tiers at 200, 500, 1,000, and 2,500; the sleep mask and lens cloth at 250. For a group below a minimum, the property's standard collection is the answer: the property orders at the minimum, the program uses what it needs, and the property keeps the remainder for the boutique or the next group. Agencies running several programs a year at the same property often order the shared pieces at the next tier and personalize each program with the card and the color. Pricing is confirmed at quote review once artwork and colors are set.

## Timeline for a program date

Production runs 4 to 6 weeks after proof approval, plus transit to the property. Working backwards from the arrival date:

| Milestone | What happens | Timing before arrival |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brief | Kit and drop contents, qualifier count, colorway, both logos in vector form, property delivery address | 10–12 weeks |
| Mockup and proof | Logo hierarchy, placements, and threads approved | Confirmed with the quote |
| Production | Pieces made and inspected | 4–6 weeks after proof approval |
| Delivery and staging | Individually wrapped pieces delivered to the property; drops assembled on site by the DMC or the concierge | 1–2 weeks |

For a short lead time, pieces that use the property's existing crest with no new digitizing proof fastest: a Petite and a Lumière in the program color can be the fastest drop in the catalog.

## Presentation

Every piece arrives individually wrapped; custom gift packaging is available for the keepsake pieces and the Petite can be tissue-lined on request. The card is where the program lives: the night's message, tomorrow's schedule, a note from the sponsor. Keep the goods looking like the property and the card carrying the program, and the gift reads as a property gift rather than a corporate one, which is the point.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the minimum order for incentive trip gifts?

Hats, leather goods, and the yoga mat from 100 units, bags and bottles from 200, the sleep mask and lens cloth from 250. Groups below a minimum order the property's standard collection and the property keeps the remainder.

### What is the best vehicle for a room drop?

The Petite Mini Tote: it holds a night's gift, stands on a bed or desk, and is used afterwards. Brief one per qualifier per drop, or one per stay with a new gift inside each night.

### Can the kit carry the property's, the sponsor's, and the agency's logos?

The property's crest takes the standard position and the sponsor's mark sits small and secondary; the agency and the trip name go on the printed card inside the Petite, not on the goods.

### How far ahead should an incentive program brief its gifts?

Brief 10 to 12 weeks before arrival. Production takes 4 to 6 weeks after proof approval; pieces using the property's existing crest proof fastest.

### Who assembles the room drops?

Pieces arrive individually wrapped at the property; the DMC or the concierge assembles the drops on site. Custom gift packaging and tissue-lined Petites are quoted alongside the goods.
